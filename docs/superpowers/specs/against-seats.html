The position worth taking

They score the student. We check whether anyone measured.

Fifty signals sounds like an advantage until you ask what happens when a signal is missing — and for most students, most signals are. A platform that quietly reads “no library loans” as low engagement is not measuring the student. It is measuring whether the college bought a library connector.

This codebase already refuses to do that, in four places, and none of it is marketed anywhere. That is the product position: not more data, better-founded data.

Null is not zero

An unmeasured figure is null and leaves the denominator. It is never scored 0, which would put an unmeasured student top of a risk list.

Components renormalise

A component with no evidence is dropped from BOTH sides of the division, not counted as a failure the student never had a chance to avoid.

Register coverage

Three marks out of sixty is a fact about a tutor, not a student. Coverage is measured, stored, and the attendance figure is withheld below the floor.

Thin evidence is flagged

A score resting on less than half the model is labelled, not published as if it were confident. The weight actually used is reported alongside it.

Why this sells, rather than merely being true

At a UKVI or funding audit the question is never “what was the score”. It is “show me the evidence behind this decision”. A number that can say which components it rests on, how much of the register was actually taken, and where it declined to judge, survives that conversation. A single opaque engagement score does not.

What to build, in order

Five moves

Ranked by what each unlocks, not by effort.

  1. Registers from the timetable, not typed dates

    Turn a published TimetableEvent into its HndSession, the way Canvas, Moodle and Arbor all do it. The CELCAT-style scheduler built on 30 August is already dated and already carries the room — its design deliberately declared itself self-contained, stating that it "does not modify HndSession … or the register generator". This is that seam, closed.

    Unlocks: fixes a real bug — the current generator steps seven days from one weekday, so a unit taught Monday and Wednesday cannot be expressed at all. Gives real times instead of the fixed 3-hour blocks (10:00–13:00, 14:00–17:00…), and carries the room, which is what would later let a tag be bound to a place. It also makes the scheduler earn its keep: today a college can build a full timetable and its registers still ignore it.

  2. QR as a second check-in method Shipped 4 Sep

    A rotating code on the lecturer’s screen. Their most-used fallback.

    Unlocks: every phone with a camera, including the ones with no NFC. And a rotating code is genuinely harder to abuse than a static sticker, which closes the one hole we have documented rather than fixed.

    Done. Merged and deployed to staging and production on 4 September: rotating signed codes (HMAC over session and window, keyed off the mandatory session secret, so no new configuration to deploy), projector mode, lecturer end-early and hold-open controls, a scanner in the student app, and method on every tap row so a disputed register can say which way the mark arrived. 107 assertions across 15 sections. It ships switched on.

  3. Missing registers, as a named screen

    Bromcom ships “Today’s Missing Registers”. The figure already exists as registersDue, buried in one profile.

    Unlocks: the coverage story above stops being a number in an engine and becomes something a manager acts on. Smallest build on this list.

  4. A small rules engine

    Threshold → notify tutor → open an Intervention. Every piece exists; nothing joins them automatically.

    Unlocks: the one stage where they are genuinely ahead. Banded letters already prove the pattern — this generalises it beyond attendance.

  5. A UKVI report

    A named, exportable return rather than a screen someone reads figures off.

    Unlocks: the thing colleges with sponsored students actually buy on, and where the evidence-quality argument lands hardest.

Deliberately not

Where not to fight

Fifty signals

They have LTI connectors to seventy campus systems and years of integration work. Racing that is losing slowly, and each thin signal weakens the evidence argument that is the actual differentiator.

Library, room booking, lecture capture, Teams

Low value until the five above exist. A signal nobody can act on is a column, not a capability.

Beacons

£15–25 per room plus batteries, for an interaction identical to a sticker costing pennies. Their “no risk of proxy attendance” claim is overstated: a BLE beacon broadcasts a fixed ID that can be cloned, exactly as ours can.

A constraint-solving scheduler

Corrected 5 September. This entry used to read "our own timetabling product — do not become a scheduler". That advice arrived too late: the CELCAT-style scheduler was designed and built on 30 August, and it is good — dated events, rooms, staff, groups, categories, draft/publish, and clash detection reported per date rather than as one lump.

So the line moves rather than disappears. What we still should not build is the half CELCAT actually sells: automatic timetable generation — constraint solving, room optimisation, "fit 300 modules into 40 rooms". Detecting a clash a human created is a feature. Solving the assignment problem is a product, and a hard one.
